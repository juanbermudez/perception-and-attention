# Accuracy audit: Vision topic and visual region guides

Scope: `pathways.ts` topic `vision` (intro, insight, caveat, steps 1–9) and `region-guides.ts` guides `retina`, `retinaR`, `chiasm`, `lgn`, `v1`, `extrastriate`, `mt`, `it`, `ffa`, `ppa`, `eba`, `vwfa`, plus `parietal.roles.vision`. I also checked the matching `regions.ts` `where` fields and the `region-anatomy.ts` highlight meshes.

Method: Crossref metadata for every cited DOI; Europe PMC abstracts; NCBI PMC full text where open (Nassi & Callaway 2009, DiCarlo et al. 2012, Grill-Spector & Weiner 2014, Pitcher & Ungerleider 2021, Pawar et al. 2024, Epstein 2008, Kravitz et al. 2013). Raw abstracts are saved in `scratchpad/research/vision-src/abstracts.json`.

## 3. Could not verify
- **Purves (NBK10885, NBK11145) and StatPearls (NBK553189)**: NCBI Bookshelf served a reCAPTCHA to both curl and WebFetch. I did not try to get around it. The claims that rely on them I checked only against standard textbook knowledge: the retina is CNS tissue derived from the diencephalon; the chiasm lies above the pituitary; the optic tract projects to the midbrain; bipolar, horizontal and amacrine cell roles.
- **Hubel & Wiesel 1959**: no abstract in Europe PMC. That V1 neurons prefer particular orientations is textbook fact; the 1959 paper is in cats.
- **Full text not open**:
  - Zihl 1983: the tea-pouring anecdote. The abstract confirms bilateral lateral temporo-occipital damage and largely lost motion vision, with some central motion vision below 10 deg/s.
  - Horton & Hoyt 1991: numbers.
  - Downing 2001: overlap with MT.
  - Dehaene & Cohen 2011: case invariance.
  - Born & Bradley 2005: pattern cells.
  - Kanwisher 1997: right-hemisphere bias. The PMC XML returned only metadata; the bias is well established elsewhere.
- **Peelen & Downing 2005, *Nat Neurosci* 8:125 (10.1038/nn0205-125a)**: a correspondence with no abstract. A secondary summary says only 19% overlap between the movement-responsive region and the EBA, but I could not confirm it, so I did not use it.
- **Midget share**: Perry 1984 gives about 80% of all ganglion cells. Nassi & Callaway 2009, citing Dacey, give about 70% of LGN-projecting cells. Estimates vary; the text is consistent with its cited source.

## 4. Side notes outside scope (not counted)
- `parietal.limit` says the highlight is "the superior parietal lobule and the intraparietal sulcus", but `region-anatomy.ts` highlights only `Superior_parietal_lobulel`. An `Intraparietal` mesh exists in the atlas but is not used.
- `scripts/place-functional-areas.mjs` / `functional-areas.json`, MT `basis`: it says MT lies where the ascending limb of the ITS meets the lateral occipital sulcus. Dumoulin 2000 actually found the **ITS–ALITS** junction to be the more reliable landmark: the LO–ALITS junction had to be interpolated in 47% of cases and was absent in 21%.
